## Critical Claim Checks

1. **Plugin-first versus plugin-only:** the memo answers the native choice directly, favors DuckDB conditionally, and does not silently replace the original comparison with a CLI-only specification.
2. **Full objective preserved:** all five GTM paths are analyzed; all seventeen architecture options remain available. Selecting an initial experiment does not assert a universal storage winner or discard structural computation.
3. **Competitive baseline:** PostgreSQL graph alternatives, DuckDB graph extensions/keyed SQL, existing public dependency answers and incumbent repair are explicit controls. No claim that the ecosystem lacks graph algorithms survives.
4. **Resource honesty:** physical RAM, host/client allocations, source cost, complete preparation/output and refresh remain inside declared boundaries. A separate remote source is not charged zero; a process cap is not a whole-machine proof.
5. **Adoption honesty:** installation, sample success, independent own-data completion, voluntary repeat, organizational adoption and revenue are separate. Public complaints and downstream company revenue are not promoted into our demand.
6. **Quantitative honesty:** the funnel's 4-versus-24 example is invented explanatory arithmetic; the 33-query break-even is a simplified model; prior Bolt measurements are historical. No new generic speedup, market-size or success probability is invented.
7. **Source freshness:** retrieval is dated; older launch narratives remain historical, mutable docs remain mutable, platform uncertainties remain stated. A failed search is not proof of absence.
8. **Research versus execution:** next-quarter code, release and customer experiments are not acceptance requirements to execute within this research goal. They are the concrete next steps the goal asks us to recommend.

These gaps bound what the recommendation claims; they do not require continuing an unbounded web survey.
