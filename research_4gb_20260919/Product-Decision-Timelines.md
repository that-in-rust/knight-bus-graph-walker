# Product Decision Timelines

Date: 2026-09-20 local. Status: conditional research strategy for the next quarter, not an implementation schedule, delivery guarantee or validated sales forecast. The weeks and months below are decision checkpoints. No new customer interview, external verification, implementation or benchmark was performed for this document.

## Decision Frame

**Which complete graph workflow should earn the next investment?** Start with a repeated, consequential dependency investigation close to the existing proof. Give structural execution an immediate opportunity to supersede it when the customer's source and question genuinely qualify. Keep batch globals available for capacity-limited jobs; pursue an embedded channel only with an accountable partner.

The outcome is a useful answer before its decision expires, followed by a useful answer after data changes, at an acceptable combined deployment cost. The buyer should not have to purchase a storage-format thesis or a collection of resource receipts.

### Shared Job Contract

These obligations apply to every timeline, including a partner deployment. Present the customer with data, question, budget, deadline and freshness choices; keep implementation accounting behind them.

- **Source to build:** consistent CSV node/edge/membership inputs or a consistent authorized Neo4j projection; preserve original IDs, isolates, required properties, direction, multiplicity and invalid-row handling. Charge extraction, validation, ID mapping, sorting, every selected index and publication. No larger hidden builder.
- **RAM and storage:** total physical RAM is at most 4,000,000,000 bytes, including colocated clients/source and OS. The provisional 3 GB worker allowance is a plan to validate, not a measurement. Prepared storage is at most 50,000,000,000 bytes across unique retained blocks, indexes, results and pinned generations. Separately agree peak workspace for inputs, unsealed builds, scratch, recovery and output. Never assume a second free 50 GB generation.
- **Query to consumer:** preserve the chosen answer, including paths versus reached sets, filters, weights, ties and numerical stopping contract. Include queuing, spill, serialization, original-ID mapping and complete consumption. A giant list-valued row can defeat a bounded client; use an agreed bounded export when appropriate, not silent truncation or API substitution. Start with one heavy stage at a time.
- **Refresh:** ask maximum answer age at delivery, source watermark, deletion/revocation urgency and useful queries before the next invalidation. Changing a seed can reuse layout; changing an omitted property or operator may require new preparation. Count failed reuse attempts, full fallback and retained readers. Snapshot-first stabilizes each computation; it does not assert that customer data is static or live analytics absent.
- **Economics:** compare retained source, extraction impact, worker/build/refresh, storage/I/O/transfer and integration/on-call cost over the same customer period. Credit only a bill, capacity purchase or operator burden actually avoided. Extra preparation pays only when its useful savings arrive before invalidation; first-answer and freshness deadlines must also hold.

The allocation/work bound and a conditional elapsed-time estimate are different promises. A safe refusal is useful behavior, but a product that refuses the important job has not won. Source: [complete workflow and customer choices](/Users/amuldotexe/Desktop/personal-repos-lane/knight-bus-graph-walker/research_4gb_20260919/End-To-End-Workflow.md:1).

### Diagnose Before Replacing

Use the customer's last failed job, not a hypothetical graph size. Distinguish:

| Finding in the replay | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Missing index, unnecessarily broad query/projection, inappropriate concurrency, client materialization, repairable ingestion bottleneck or version-specific estimator defect | Try the smallest supported correction. If it restores the useful workflow within budget, close the replacement opportunity for that episode. A false estimate is not a demonstrated physical capacity floor; bypassing a guard is not the repair proof. |
| Matching compressed projection plus required algorithm/output state still exceeds available memory, or repeated preparation forces unacceptable capacity spending | A genuine capacity experiment is warranted, provided the job fits the separate prepared/output constraints and has a useful deadline. |
| The graph fits, but licensing or operating a separate service dominates | Evaluate consolidation or the partner route. This may be a real buying problem without validating our low-RAM mechanism. |

These are diagnostic branches, not diagnoses of current issue reporters. The previously checked large-projection report supports technical pain but also records weak enterprise demand for spill; its roughly 4 TB stored graph is not evidence of a fit within our 50 GB prepared allowance. ## Timeline A: Snapshot Investigation

**Opening move:** reproduce a release-impact or dependency investigation with multiple useful seed queries against a versioned artifact. This is the best-evidenced starting experiment, not yet the biggest demonstrated capacity opportunity.

**People.** A platform/devtools or security engineering lead owns the budget and missed-decision consequence. A data/platform engineer operates extraction and refresh. A developer, analyst or reviewer consumes the affected-item list or explicitly requested witness to decide what to inspect or fix. They need not be the same person.

**First week decision.** Obtain one authorized input, its actual queries, a changed version and an answer reviewer. Establish first-answer deadline, maximum source age, queries per version and the workaround attempted. Prefer a question inside the existing endpoint profile; a required witness is a declared extension. Test incumbent repair first. Reject the assumption that a dependency edge alone represents an effective access permission.

**First month decision.** Evaluate one complete replay: CSV/Neo4j capture -> bounded ID/property normalization and directed typed layout -> publish -> run the actual seed/filter mix -> deliver full original-ID results to the review tool -> rebuild a changed generation and repeat. D01/D02/D03 are the core research options. Inspect cold first-answer time as well as warm interaction. Do not add D04/D05 until repeated snapshots expose a worthwhile recomputation cost.

**Quarter decision.** Expand the supported question only if the operator voluntarily uses the workflow again and the consumer acts on the result. Choose whether the next investment is a needed witness/filter, cheaper refresh, or more capacity. Require an accountable adoption decision; a second favorable demo is not a purchase. Retain the source system during evaluation and make rollback an ordinary workflow choice.

**Contract and lived experience.** Reachability, minimum-hop distance, one witness and all paths are different products. Negative answers require complete search under the declared predicates. Security snapshots may support investigation while being unsuitable for live enforcement. The analyst gains predictable access to a stable version, but may wait behind serial builds; the operator gains another export and stale-data responsibility. UI mapping and source permissions can cost more effort than the narrow Bolt adapter.

**Upside / downside / reversibility.** Most direct route from existing proof to testing useful adoption; potentially repeatable release or investigative work. It can also reveal that the warm kernel saving is too small to matter or that richer semantics dominate. Reversibility is high while the source and downstream workflow remain intact; lower after custom query migration.

**Kill criteria.** Stop this episode if a supported incumbent repair suffices; exact required permissions/path semantics are outside the chosen contract; freshness cannot tolerate capture/build/queue time; full build/output/refresh exceeds the envelope; or no recurring decision and owner emerge. A narrow successful endpoint benchmark does not override any of these failures.

## Timeline B: Structurally Factored Analytics

**Opening move:** find a real question whose source already contains exact memberships or other admissible repeated structure. D06 avoids derived edges; D16 additionally eliminates entity-sized iterative rank state; D17 may make the next solve cheaper. They solve different bottlenecks and are not an obligatory three-feature bundle.

**People.** A fraud/security/analytics product owner buys a useful prioritization or relationship analysis. A data engineer owns membership quality and versions. An investigator or analyst consumes candidates, distances, witnesses or scores. A graph-shaped dataset alone is not evidence that PageRank changes their decision.

**First week decision.** Inspect an actual incidence export and requested operator. Measure entity count N, feature count F, memberships, group skew, useful query variations and refresh frequency during bounded profiling. Select D06 traversal/connectivity if that answers the task. Select D16 only for symmetric shared-feature-count weighted PageRank without self edges, with the declared personalization/dangling and stationary-error semantics. Binary 'shares any feature', arbitrary pair filters and general directed graphs do not inherit D16 equivalence. F much smaller than N is an opportunity signal, not proof of a complete fit.

**First month decision.** Compare source -> external membership validation/deduplication/counting/ID mapping -> selected incidence layout -> algorithm execution -> full requested original-ID answer against a competent incidence-aware baseline. Use a small independent expanded oracle for meaning, not a giant expansion as the only competitor. With D16, keep two feature vectors, never materialize dense K, charge every row scan and reconstruct entity scores. A changed damping or personalization query reuses topology but is not a free cached answer.

**Quarter decision.** Promote D17 only if repeated membership changes and fixed entity/feature universes, IDs, damping and personalization match the actual job. Discover all affected old/new rows through charged postings or a scan; update the feature residual including global dangling correction; either reconstruct against the new snapshot or execute the bounded further solve. Compare the second complete answer with full D16 recomputation, including a failed reuse gate. Outside that refresh profile, use a newly admitted solve, not an improvised incremental claim.

**Contract and lived experience.** D16's stationary L1 accuracy is not GDS finite-iteration parity. Normalization and top-k ordering need their own justified output treatment. D17 reuses solver state, not generally old scores; one membership edit may affect a large group. The operator trades expanded topology and rank arrays for stricter data-model ownership, numerical care and update discovery. The consumer should still receive a familiar useful answer, not learn the algebra.

**Resource stress test.** D16's favorable constructed example has 1B entities, 1M features and 2B memberships. Its named portfolio is about 36.032 GB before omitted metadata and other costs; adding D17 reverse postings brings it to about 44.040 GB. A failed gate requiring an additional 8 GB score plane crosses the 50 GB retained cap before overhead. Omit optional retained scores, share eligible blocks or agree a smaller/serial retention plan in advance. Even passing reuse cannot erase an N-row export. At damping 0.85 with zero initialization, the example's sufficient 113 updates for the 1e-8 L1 bound move 2.26 TB of row payload, about 4,520 seconds at an assumed 500 MB/s before build/output: arithmetic, not a measured one-hour service.

**Upside / downside / reversibility.** Highest conditional upside because it can avoid both expanded work and mutable state, without identical entity rows. Highest semantic/model risk of the paths: an impressive result for the wrong projection is useless. Reversibility is medium if canonical source memberships and IDs remain available; lower if customers start depending on operator-specific saved answers. Likelihood depends primarily on finding relevant structure and real use, not the size of the synthetic ratio.

**Kill criteria.** Do not promote when exact incidence is unavailable or expensive to recover; F approaches N without another benefit; the requested projection differs; scans/output miss the deadline; simpler degree/frequency priorities are equally useful; or fallback cannot fit. Kill D17 separately when change coverage is incomplete, affected-row/index costs erase the second-run gain, or numerical error cannot support the requested contract. These failures need not kill D06 or D16.

## Timeline C: Budgeted Batch Globals

**Opening move:** take a global job that cannot economically finish today, whose consumer accepts a scheduled answer. Select the family from that job; do not choose PageRank because its implementation is familiar.

**People.** An analytics/platform lead funds compute and missed deadlines. A data engineer operates scheduled capture and recovery. A domain analyst or downstream application consumes a component report, similarity candidates, scores, communities or embeddings and owns the usefulness criterion.

**First week decision.** Establish the next real decision window, tolerated age, run frequency and required output. Calculate the irreducible output/state footprint. Compare a tuned incumbent, an eligible ephemeral analytics session and a simpler SQL/custom or approximate answer where the consumer permits it. Choose the hard family that actually blocks this buyer. Exact similarity is a valuable architecture stress test, not automatically a demand winner.

**First month decision.** Evaluate CSV/Neo4j capture -> bounded external normalization and scan-friendly storage -> one admitted global job with external state/spills -> full output to the report/training/application sink -> the next scheduled snapshot and result. D08 handles one PageRank state regime; D11/D12 address exact similarity; D14 addresses a specified embedding recurrence. Connectivity and D15 community work remain distinct options. Count every pass, sort, candidate spool, convergence step and any writeback to the retained source.

**Quarter decision.** If both first and changed-data runs finish before their useful deadlines, trial recurring operation and assess avoidable cost per useful job. Choose either more families or improved refresh only from actual demand. If a longer run still lowers cost and meets the deadline, keep it without calling it a speed win. If schedules slip into a growing stale backlog, change the plan or stop.

**Contract and lived experience.** A component partition is not directed reachability. PageRank weights, sinks, scaling and stopping must match the chosen oracle. Exact similarity needs complete candidates, self exclusion, ties, zero tails and filter semantics; small top-k output does not imply little work. Community quality and embedding utility require their own acceptance criteria. Operations shift from interactive failures to overnight disk, duration and recovery concerns; the next morning's usable answer matters more than a small resident-state number.

**Upside / downside / reversibility.** Broader capacity applicability on nonredundant graphs, with potential to avoid a larger always-on analytics host. Longer runtime, storage traffic, retries and retained source costs may erase savings. Reversibility is high while inputs/outputs remain portable scheduled artifacts; it falls with proprietary downstream models or writeback dependencies. Near-term commercial likelihood is uncertain until one owner chooses the slower-but-useful tradeoff.

**Kill criteria.** Required output alone cannot fit or reach its sink; unbounded candidate growth or near-one-damping work misses the deadline; agreed approximation/simple methods already suffice; claimed savings disappear after retained source and added worker/ops costs without compensating capacity value; or existing disk-oriented/custom tools complete the same task more simply. Do not retain a batch path solely because it avoids OOM.

## Timeline D: Embedded Partner Channel

**Opening move:** let an existing security/data product deliver one A, B or C workflow inside its current customer experience. This is a distinct distribution and support decision, not another engine architecture or a presumption that embedded is always cheaper.

**People.** The partner's product/engineering owner is the immediate buyer and release sponsor. Its platform team operates deployment, upgrades and tenancy. Its customers' analysts consume answers through the existing UI/API. End-customer value and partner margin/support burden must both improve.

**First week decision.** Ask one interested practitioner for a named product workflow, artifact/change trace, engineering owner and integration review slot. Compare in-process embedding with a bounded subprocess/sidecar and the partner's current stack. Proceed only when removing a specific operational or capacity problem matters more than merely adding 'graph support'.

**First month decision.** Specify partner-controlled CSV/Neo4j export -> worker build inside the deployment budget -> narrow supported calls -> complete original-ID results in the partner's consumer -> partner-owned refresh/publication and version rollback. Reuse the tested Bolt profile only where it matches; a native interface may be better for large streamed output. Demonstrate one integration slice when feasible, rather than designing a general plugin platform first.

**Quarter decision.** Seek repeated use through the partner's release process, agreed support responsibilities and a concrete commercial adoption decision. Expand only after observing customer benefit. Partner enthusiasm without data, engineering time or a release commitment does not justify a quarter of platform work.

**Contract and lived experience.** Tenant isolation, cancellation, upgrade/version behavior and source credentials become part of the boundary. Embedded memory is not free: partner host, consumer, worker and OS share 4 GB if colocated. A remote source retains its separate cost. Refresh must coordinate pinned readers and partner rollback without duplicating unlimited generations. The end user may see less change, while the partner inherits another dependency and engineers face longer diagnosis across ownership boundaries.

**Upside / downside / reversibility.** Existing distribution and workflow can lower direct-selling and UI friction. Partner release cadence, licensing review, support and custom requests can instead consume the quarter; platform consolidation may make any new component unattractive. Reversibility is medium before a release and lower after persisted formats/API contracts enter customer deployments. The existing practitioner signal makes a scoped conversation plausible, not a paid deal likely enough to forecast.

**Kill criteria.** No accountable integration owner or real artifact; a demand for broad transactional/Cypher replacement before the useful slice; partner+worker cannot fit together; support/integration cost exceeds realized benefit; or the partner's existing embedded/relational alternative already meets the need. Preserve portable inputs and outputs so stopping does not strand the customer.

The robust investment across paths is a minimum complete import-to-answer-to-refresh slice, not all infrastructure imaginable. Carry an independent answer oracle and essential failure/resource checks with that slice; automate the small operational record. Do not turn proof administration, exhaustive interface parity or a universal planner into prerequisites for learning whether anyone needs the answer.

**Preserve the portfolio.** This document changes no decision-map option. D01/D02 support all paths; D03-D05 support investigation/reuse; D06/D07/D10/D16/D17 offer distinct structural routes; D08/D09/D11/D12/D14/D15 remain global-state, numerical, similarity, embedding and community options; D13 remains restricted factorized-query expansion. The partner channel can carry any qualifying route. Weighted paths, connectivity, motifs and other families remain in the source-ledger agenda. An experiment order is not a smaller research goal or a universal format choice.

## Decision Filter

**First experiment: one real dependency investigation, twice, with an incumbent-repair control.** Start from the existing narrow Bolt profile when it answers the question. Obtain a baseline and changed source artifact, several real seed/filter requests, the full consumer output and an owner-agreed deadline, freshness limit and avoidable-cost target. Run the whole source/build/query/output/refresh workflow under the declared RAM, retained-storage and workspace limits. Report actual counts, encoded bytes and the failing stage; a below-RAM integration replay is not a capacity demonstration. This extends proof we have while testing the largest product unknowns: whether the answer matters and whether preparation/refresh erase the gain.

The first-week branch is deliberate: inspect source incidence while acquiring that episode. If the customer actually needs a compatible derived-graph job and expansion or entity-state size is the dominant bottleneck, move the first scale experiment to D06 or D16 immediately. D17 becomes the second-run comparison only when its fixed-parameter refresh profile applies. Neither PageRank familiarity nor a spectacular constructed graph earns that choice.

Set the decision rule with the owner before timing: the complete answer must be correct under the chosen contract, stay within every declared budget, arrive before it expires, and remove a material failed-job, cost or operator burden. Compare against the repaired incumbent and one relevant simple/factor-aware alternative; add other comparators only when they can change the decision. Record unsuccessful jobs as well as successes. A material threshold belongs to the buyer's consequence, not a fabricated universal speedup.

If no real episode or accountable owner emerges during the first-month discovery checkpoint, report that product evidence is missing. Keep any technical experiment explicitly technical; do not relabel synthetic success, source spending, a certificate or positive feedback as adoption. The quarter should end with a supported continue/pivot/stop decision, not a broader collection of untested promises.
