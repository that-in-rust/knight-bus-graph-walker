## Substantive Findings

These are observations about historical assistant assertions. They do not independently verify the linked external sources or adopt the historical claims as current facts. Tool-result readers own the underlying captured evidence.

1. P0006-P0052 repeatedly separates blocked HTML fetches from successful alternatives and quote verification from broader inference. However, it calls multiple pages from the same vendor independent corroboration. They can corroborate that vendor's documentation but do not constitute independent customer demand or field measurements. Historical database GB-hours, session GB-minutes, minimum durations and pause policies must not be merged into one current price claim.
2. P0059-P0119 includes claims of public rate-card discovery, corrected currency/tier arithmetic and the crucial distinction between beyond-RAM storage and beyond-RAM algorithm working state. A release-note keyword census cannot establish that no other feature, product or implementation exists. The original source's self-report of complete code verification needs the actual tool result, not its confident summary.
3. P0132-P0139 exposes scope and outcome confounds: an OLTP-oriented Reddit opinion is not evidence that analytics are the only valuable graph workload; inaccessible comments remain unknown. A historical NodeSimilarity estimator issue reportedly blocked a tiny graph yet ran quickly after bypassing the estimator. This motivates testing correct admission against real allocations, not treating every failed estimate as a graph-capacity opportunity. The reported 30 ms is historical anecdote, not a benchmark run here.
4. P0175-P0191 narrows a no-spill claim to dated code/docs and distinguishes packed off-heap adjacency from disk execution. It also acknowledges that public evidence cannot determine internal roadmaps. These caveats conflict with stronger empty-market language elsewhere in the same archive.
5. P0196-P0202 and P0244-P0250 identify algorithm breadth and a distribution channel as meaningful Onager competition while conceding early-development caveats. Rolling downloads, stars and a README checklist do not prove completed analytics jobs, paid adoption or hard RAM behavior. An in-process extension cannot be assumed to inherit its host's spill machinery, but lack of a README bullet alone cannot prove every memory property either.
6. P0207-P0229 contains conflicting rate-card access accounts and distinguishes a historical heap-default configuration issue from a GDS implementation defect. A source badge supports only the displayed affiliation; a teaser cannot establish which later product was intended. These details strengthen the need to try a configuration/query repair before treating an incident as a rewrite opportunity.
7. P0233-P0241 correctly rejects the inference that one vendor's deprioritization proves empty competitive territory. Its own final phrasing then overstates limited requests from that vendor's customers as nobody asking. Neither broad demand conclusion follows. Read the quote's population and date literally, and obtain a real customer episode.
8. P0273 explicitly calls a rate card fully read while admitting that much of it was inspected only by structure. That is not full substantive row coverage. This run preserves the distinction and does not inherit that claim. P0282 again treats optional vector support as necessarily increasing memory pressure, which is not true for a projection that omits those properties.
9. P0293-P0298 finds an actual duplicate-discovery error: the supposedly new 73039 forum evidence already occurs in `simulation01.md`. Repeated inclusion and multiple votes do not increase the number of independent incidents. New detail within an old thread must be separately identified, not renamed as a new customer source.
10. P0300-P0320 begins legacy-mode and Infinigraph qualification. Its code-read and full-thread claims remain historical self-reports. The architectural scope must distinguish topology, properties, OLTP and analytical projection before using a very-large-dataset headline as either a threat or proof of a low-RAM opportunity.
11. P0321-P0364 resolves some previously garbled issue descriptions and gives the most substantive narrative counterclaim about Ladybug's on-disk graph scanner. Its disk-projection statement is narrower than a whole-algorithm working-state cap. Finding no in-memory-graph counterpart filename does not exclude an algorithm-specific CSR, auxiliary vectors or materialized result. The full algorithm-specific evidence in the other lanes remains necessary.
12. P0337-P0341's exact versioned price feed resolves earlier claims that the SPA source was inherently unverifiable. The historical access discrepancy is method-dependent, not evidence of vendor concealment. Tier eligibility, minimum duration, currency and product must accompany any price arithmetic; these July values are not reverified September prices.
13. P0401 identifies a downstream pandas filtering issue as the cause of a reported GraphRAG entity loss, while its opening sentence loosely blames Leiden. Preserve the root-cause distinction. A proposed warning threshold is not an observed loss rate, and a batched issue closure does not establish a maintainer's technical judgment.
14. P0421-P0463 demonstrates a search-instrument failure: a vendor/community index can return exact zero counts while omitting body text and multimedia. The narrative supplies a concrete allegedly missed GraphRAG page and negative controls for other database names. Thus zero hits do not establish zero industry use or demand. Reproducing a flawed instrument's counts is not a successful verification of the inference.
15. P0464-P0480 gives competing explanations for procurement increases: unchanged descriptions, undisclosed scope, reseller fees, missing later awards and a different agency's lower price. The correct conclusion is that matched capability and negotiated price remain unknown. Missing keyword-matched awards do not prove churn, another purchase does not prove replacement, and government record fields cannot prove no private negotiation occurred.
16. P0490-P0533 qualifies review-market evidence: small samples, free users, credits, individuals and employer-size tags are not enterprise procurement testimony. Zero pricing complaints in nine reviews does not prove no pain. These are discovery leads, not a statistically representative or causal customer study.
17. P0534-P0626 repeatedly distinguishes property sharding, analytical sessions, core-priced self-managed licenses, and database tiers. The presence of a RAM value in one configured license bundle does not establish proportional RAM-based license pricing. A zero-value order may reflect de-obligation rather than a free deployment. Distinct products cannot be combined into a supposed single incumbent bill.
18. P0627-P0674 separates award magnitude from pain and notes an actual-outlay field in one reported record. Even if accurate, an intermediary's bundled renewal payment does not identify the cost of graph algorithms or willingness to switch to this engine. A multi-year warranty/perpetual-license description cannot be silently treated as annual recurring revenue. Upstream/downstream government systems are not independent observations of separate purchases.
19. P0675-P0712 contains useful scope control for capacity economics: an AWS managed analytical SKU and a self-hosted machine of similar RAM have different prices and services. Discrete capacity ladders also mean a percentage reduction in memory need not produce the same percentage bill reduction. Its repeated same-SKU procurement language still admits missing configuration detail; the caveat must override stronger conclusions elsewhere.
20. P0713 explicitly identifies a migration story as Dgraph rather than Neo4j, with CPU as trigger and lower cost as outcome. Do not relabel this as direct evidence that Neo4j/GDS customers need low-RAM algorithms. It can still inspire a hypothesis about the customer's real decision trigger.
21. P0714-P0829 repeatedly disputes whether a new shared-infrastructure tier is a price cut to an existing enterprise contract. Some extractor summaries still claim a strongest-possible revealed-preference signal after their own verifiers identify different services and unknown negotiated baselines. Use the qualified record, not the most emphatic summary. Stable sampled list prices likewise do not disprove discounts or buyer pressure.
22. P0787-P0793 says analytical sessions predate the forum comment used as a causal timeline anchor. Even without adopting the dates as current fact, the archive does not establish that a launch was a response to that comment or to pressure from a particular customer class.
23. P0830-P0872 returns to configured license bundles, property-only sharding, price snapshots and aggregate procurement. These narratives add no algorithm benchmark or authorized replayable customer dataset. P0848's equation of graph-shard topology with GDS projection constraints is too compressed: they are different subsystems and require their own execution evidence. Totals and announcements do not establish which workload our proposed engine can replace.
